# Research

Enough to design with conviction. Not a literature review.

## The category

Before the client, the category. You need to know:

- What everyone in it looks like, so you can tell when you are producing the average
- What the conventions actually do - some are clichés, some are load-bearing. An
  aerospace supplier's certifications block is not a cliché, it is the proof.
- The vocabulary insiders use. Using the outsider's word for something is an instant tell.
- What the buyer is afraid of. Deep-tech buyers fear unproven; consumer buyers fear
  wasting money; B2B buyers fear looking stupid internally.

Read three real companies in the category properly, not their homepages only. Read a
pricing page, a docs page, a careers page - that is where the real voice is.

## Reference points

Three, named, with what specifically is taken from each. Not "Stripe" - "Stripe's
restraint with colour: one accent, everything else neutral".

Pick at least one reference from outside the category. A deep-tech site that borrows
from scientific publishing beats one that borrows from three other deep-tech sites.

## The clichés list

Write down what everyone in this category does that you are refusing. Be specific
enough that someone could check the output against it.

Examples of what a real list looks like:

- SaaS: purple-to-blue gradient, floating dashboard screenshot at a 15 degree angle,
  "Trusted by" logo wall above the fold, three feature cards with line icons
- Deep tech: dark blue, hexagon patterns, circuit-board textures, generic lab stock photo
- Consumer wellness: sans-serif on cream, single leaf illustration, "clean" and "pure"
- Fintech: isometric illustration, phone mockup at an angle, gradient mesh blob

## Tools

- `design-extractor` pulls a system out of a live URL - use it on your references to
  get their actual tokens rather than guessing.
- `competitor-experience-audit` if installed, for a structured pass over rivals.
- WebSearch for the category, the buyer's job title, and the objection.

## Exit

Three references with specifics, a clichés list, and one sentence on what the category
gets wrong that you are going to get right. If that last sentence is weak, the project
will be too.
