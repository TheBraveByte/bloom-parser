# Brief intake

The brief you write, not the one you were given.

## The seven answers

You need all seven before phase 2. Ask only for what you cannot reasonably infer, and
ask in one message rather than seven.

1. **What is it.** One sentence, no adjectives. "An observability platform for
   infrastructure teams" not "a cutting-edge AI-powered platform".
2. **Who is it for.** A specific person with a job, not a segment. "The one engineer on
   a four-person team who gets paged at 3am" beats "enterprise DevOps teams".
3. **What do they use now.** Including "a spreadsheet" and "nothing". This is where the
   real competition lives.
4. **What has to change.** The single thing someone should feel, believe or do
   differently after seeing this.
5. **Why would they not.** The objection. Design that ignores the objection is
   decoration.
6. **What is the deliverable.** A file, a running site, a document. Be specific -
   `.pdf` at final trim size, or a Vue project, or a `.pptx`.
7. **What is fixed.** Existing brand, existing tech, a locked colour, a legal
   constraint, a deadline.

## Then write the positioning

One sentence, this shape:

> For [specific person], [name] is the [category] that [single differentiator],
> because [the reason that is credible].

If the differentiator could be claimed by three competitors, it is not one. Go back.

## And the refusal

Write what this piece is deliberately not. Two or three lines. This is the most useful
paragraph in the brief because it is the one that gets checked against the output.

## For concept and portfolio projects

Same seven answers. Invent the company, research the category honestly. The positioning
has to be one a real company in that category could defend, or the piece reads as a
render rather than design.

Record that it is self-initiated in the brief itself, so the case study cannot
accidentally imply otherwise.

## Red flags in a brief

- "Make it pop", "modern", "clean", "premium" with nothing behind them - these are
  requests for reassurance, not direction. Convert them into a named reference.
- A list of features with no hierarchy - someone has not decided what matters.
- "Like [competitor] but better" - find out which part, specifically.
- A logo request with no positioning - the logo is the last step, not the first.
