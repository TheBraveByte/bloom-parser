# Case study

The project is not finished until it is written up. A gallery of screenshots is not a
portfolio - it shows the output and hides the thinking, and the thinking is what gets
hired.

## Structure

```
Title            The name, and what it is, in under ten words
Label            Self-initiated concept project, or the real client and scope
Role             What you actually did. Be exact.
Deliverables     The list, with formats
Timeframe        Honest

The brief        What was being asked, in one paragraph
The category     What everyone in it does, and why that fails
The problem      The real one, which is rarely the stated one

Direction        The three you generated, the one you picked, why
The system       BRAND.md or DESIGN.md, shown as decisions not swatches
The structure    Information architecture, narrative order
The build        What it is made of, and the one hard technical part
The signature    The memorable thing, and why it earns its place

What changed     What you got wrong first, and what fixed it
Result           What the piece does now. Measured if you can measure it.
```

## Rules

- **Show the rejected directions.** Two dead ends with reasons is more convincing than
  five polished screens.
- **Show one wrong turn.** Real projects have them. A case study with none reads as
  fiction.
- **Label concept work as concept work.** "Self-initiated concept project exploring how
  an advanced-materials company could communicate complex technology" is credible and
  costs you nothing. Implying a client you did not have costs you everything the moment
  someone asks about it.
- **Be exact about your role.** "Brand, web design and frontend build" not "designed and
  developed". If you generated imagery with a model, say so - it is a skill now, not a
  confession.
- **No unsupported claims.** No invented conversion numbers. If there is no metric, write
  about the decision instead.
- **Screenshots at real size, in context.** A mobile screen inside a phone frame, a
  poster on a wall, a package as a package. Flat crops of a hero section prove nothing.

## What makes one good

The difference between a good case study and a gallery is that a good one could be read
with the images removed and still make sense. Write it that way, then add the images.

The line that does the most work is usually the refusal - what you decided not to do,
and why. That is the sentence a client reads and thinks this person makes decisions.

## Length

Enough to carry the thinking, short enough to be read. Roughly:

- Brief, category, problem - three short paragraphs
- Direction - one paragraph plus the three named options
- System and structure - shown, with captions that give reasons
- Build - one paragraph, plus the hard part
- What changed - one honest paragraph
- Result - two sentences

Anything longer is a process document, not a case study.
