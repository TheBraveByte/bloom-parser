# 06 - NOMA

Self-initiated concept project.

**Proves:** consumer identity, packaging, art direction, physical-world design.

## The fiction

NOMA is a premium supplement brand. The reference points are Aesop, Muji, a good
pharmacy and Japanese editorial design - not sports nutrition.

## The hard constraint, stated first

**This project makes no health claims.** Not in the copy, not on the packaging, not in
the case study. No claims about what the product does to a body, no implied outcomes, no
invented clinical language, no fake study citations.

The design problem becomes: how does a package communicate quality, care and restraint
without saying what is inside does anything. That is a more interesting problem than the
one most supplement brands solve, and it is the reason this piece is worth making.

Copy is about the object, the ritual, the sourcing and the format. Not the effect.

## Category clichés to refuse

Amber glass with a kraft label, a single leaf illustration, "clean" and "pure",
before-and-after imagery, a chalkboard font, a muscular arm, sans-serif on cream with
wide tracking, the word "wellness".

## Deliverables

Physical:
- Primary container - form, material, closure, label
- Secondary carton
- Shipping box and what opening it is like
- Insert or instruction card
- A set, so the system is visible across three products not one

Digital:
- `BRAND.md`
- Wordmark as `.svg`
- Brand guidelines `.pdf` - `canvas-design`
- Site - `vue-web-builder`
- Product photography direction, then generated imagery following it
- Social set
- Launch campaign - three pieces that work together

## Notes on execution

Design the dieline, not just the label artwork. A flat rectangle labelled "packaging" is
the tell that separates packaging design from graphic design applied to a photo of a box.

Print reality: CMYK, bleed, safe margins, and a note on substrate and finish. State
whether that colour is achievable in the specified print process - a brand colour that
only exists in RGB is a mistake found at the printer.

Mockups in context. On a shelf next to competitors, held in a hand, opened on a counter.
Higgsfield for environment and product imagery - keep lighting direction and grade
consistent across the whole set or it reads as a collage.

Typography carries this one almost entirely. One display face, one workhorse, and
restraint. The ingredient list and the small print are design surfaces, not leftovers -
that is where premium packaging actually shows.
